Squaring introduces a cross term
(a + b)² = a² + b² + 2ab

Here a = ŷᵢ − ȳ and b = yᵢ − ŷᵢ. A single observation usually does not satisfy total² = model² + residual².

Displayed numbers are rounded; the identities hold at full precision.

Ask the class

At point 3, the fitted value is below the mean but the observation is above the fitted value. What sign must the residual have?

Reveal the answer

Positive. The downward model contribution is partly offset by the upward residual. Squaring without the cross term loses this cancellation.

The cross terms cancel at the OLS fit.

Add the squared identity over all observations. Move the line to see which equalities depend on fitting by least squares.

Try it

Change only the slope. The line still passes through (x̄, ȳ), so residuals still sum to zero. Does that make the cross term zero?

Reveal the answer

No. Passing through the mean is not enough to guarantee the decomposition. OLS also makes ∑xᵢêᵢ = 0. Some other lines, including the mean-only line, happen to have a zero cross term too; a zero cross term alone does not identify the OLS solution.

Experiment with a candidate line
gᵢ = ȳ + δ + b(xᵢ − x̄)

At OLS, δ = 0 and b = β̂₁. Away from OLS, use gᵢ for the candidate fitted value.

Build the proof · OLS with an intercept
SYY = SSreg + RSS + C

Expand every square and add.

C = 2∑(ŷᵢ − ȳ)êᵢ
= 2[(β̂₀ − ȳ)∑êᵢ + β̂₁∑xᵢêᵢ]
∑êᵢ = 0   and   ∑xᵢêᵢ = 0

These are the two OLS normal equations: the derivatives of RSS with respect to intercept and slope are zero.

C = 0 → SYY = SSreg + RSS

This is an algebraic property of OLS with an intercept. Normal errors are not needed.

R² is a fraction of the total variation.

Change the trend and scatter, then watch OLS allocate the sum of squares. All three sums are measured in squared Y units.

Mean square = sum of squares / degrees of freedom (df). The total mean square is the sample variance of Y.

Constructed data: yᵢ = 5 + slope × (xᵢ − 5) + scale × rᵢ. The fixed rᵢ values sum to zero and are orthogonal to X, so the controls separate trend from scatter exactly.

R² = SSreg / SYY
= 1 − RSS / SYY = r²
Adjusted R² = 1 − [RSS / (n − 2)] / [SYY / (n − 1)]

Why divide by n − 2?

Fitting the intercept and slope imposes two independent constraints on n residuals. There are n − 2 residual degrees of freedom.

Assumptions behind unbiasedness

Conditional on X, a correct linear mean and uncorrelated errors with common variance σ² give E(RSS | X) = (n − 2)σ². Normality is not required for unbiasedness; independent Gaussian errors additionally give RSS/σ² ∼ χ²(n − 2).

R² has no direction

Reverse the slope. The sign of r changes; R² does not.

A fit statistic is not causation

R² describes the observed sample fit. It does not establish a causal effect or guarantee prediction on new data.

Constant Y is a special case

If SYY = 0, there is no observed variation to explain. R² and correlation are undefined.

A precise mean can coexist with noisy predictions.

The heights example has a clearly positive slope, yet most of the variation in daughters’ heights remains unexplained.

Ask the class

Would a much larger sample remove the wide prediction interval?

Reveal the answer

It can reduce uncertainty about the mean. It cannot remove the individual variation σ². The extra “1” in √(1 + h*) remains.

Teaching guide, notation & source

Suggested 20–25 minute sequence: 4 minutes splitting a deviation; 7 minutes predicting and checking cross-term cancellation; 6 minutes exploring R², the ANOVA table, and degrees of freedom; 5 minutes comparing inference with prediction. Ask students to predict the result before moving a control. Each panel includes a question with a hidden answer.

Notation: SYY (also called SST or TSS) = ∑(yᵢ − ȳ)²; SSreg = ∑(ŷᵢ − ȳ)²; RSS (also called SSE) = ∑(yᵢ − ŷᵢ)². The identity SYY = SSreg + RSS and R² = r² apply to the OLS simple linear regression fit with an intercept and nonconstant X; R² also requires SYY > 0. The first two panels use a fixed, constructed nine-point sample. Panel 3 uses a separate constructed sample.

Interpretation: “Explained” refers to variation captured by the fitted line. R² = 1 indicates a perfect sample fit; it does not by itself establish overfitting. Adjusted R² can be negative. These clarifications keep the numerical demonstration separate from claims requiring a statistical model.
