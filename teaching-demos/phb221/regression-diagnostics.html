Topic 8 · Regression diagnostics

Leverage, outliers & influence

UCSD · FMPH 221

Three questions about one observation.

Leverage

Is its X unusual?

A point has high leverage when its predictor values are far from the rest. Leverage depends only on X and describes the potential to change the fit.

Outlier

Is its Y unusual for that X?

A regression outlier departs from the model followed by the other observations. Judge Y relative to the expected response at that X.

Influence

Does it change the fitted model?

A point is influential when leaving it out substantially changes the coefficients or fitted values. Influence describes its actual effect on the fit.

What do residuals and Cook’s distance measure?

Residual: the vertical gap

The observed response minus its fitted value. The dotted orange segment shows this gap to the line fitted with the point.

ei = yi − ŷi

A high-leverage point can pull the fitted line toward itself, making its raw residual look smaller.

Studentized residual: a scaled gap

Adjusts the residual for its expected variability. The error scale is estimated from the other observations.

r(i) = ei / [s(−i) √(1 − hi)]

Larger |r| suggests a more unusual response. A formal outlier test also accounts for checking multiple observations.

Leverage: distance in predictor space

For one predictor and an intercept:

hi = 1/n + (xi − x̄)² / Σj(xj − x̄)²

All n = 21 observations enter this calculation. With several predictors, leverage reflects an unusual combination of predictor values. Changing only Y leaves h unchanged.

Cook’s distance: change after leaving one out

Summarizes how much all fitted values change when the selected observation is omitted from the fit.

Di = Σj(ŷj − ŷj(−i))² / (2s²)

The denominator uses 2 fitted coefficients and the full-model error variance s². Predictions are compared at all 21 X values. Lecture 8 uses D > 1 as a flag for review, not a statistical test.

Other concepts from Lecture 8
ConceptMeaningWhat to look for
NonlinearityThe mean response has a shape that the fitted model misses.A curved pattern in residuals versus fitted values or a predictor.
HeteroscedasticityThe error variance changes across observations.A fan or changing spread in the residual plot.
Normality of errorsThe model errors follow a normal distribution.Systematic departures from the reference line in a suitable Q–Q plot. Lecture 8 compares studentized residuals with t quantiles.

When a point stands out: flag it, check the original data, and investigate whether the model needs revision. Automatic deletion can discard useful information.
