What does the intercept mean?

The intercept is the predicted mean when every regressor is zero. Here, ln(Miles) = 0 means Miles = 1. Zero tax, zero licensing, and zero income make this intercept difficult to interpret substantively. Centering predictors gives the intercept a more useful reference profile.

Income is measured in thousands of dollars. Its coefficient has units of gallons per person per $1,000. Changing tax moves both profiles together, so the income difference stays the same in this additive model.

A causal interpretation requires an appropriate study design and assumptions. The fitted state-level association alone does not establish that increasing income reduces fuel use.

Why are the fitted values unchanged?

Define G2–9 = WT9 − WT2 and G9–18 = WT18 − WT9. Substitute WT9 = WT2 + G2–9 and WT18 = WT2 + G2–9 + G9–18 into the original model.

β₀ + β₂ WT2 + β₉ WT9 + β₁₈ WT18
= β₀ + (β₂ + β₉ + β₁₈) WT2 + (β₉ + β₁₈) G2–9 + β₁₈ G9–18

Both parameterizations give the same fitted BMI for any weight profile, with identical residuals and R². Each WT2 coefficient answers a different conditional question.

Predictor overlap makes individual slopes less precise.

What happens if we drop a predictor?

The coefficient now describes a different comparison. Suppose E(Y | X₁, X₂) = β₀ + β₁X₁ + β₂X₂ and E(X₂ | X₁) = γ₀ + γ₁X₁. Averaging over X₂ gives:

E(Y | X₁) = (β₀ + β₂γ₀) + (β₁ + β₂γ₁)X₁

The slope becomes β₁ + β₂γ₁. Its sign can change. If X₁ and X₂ are independent, the slope remains β₁, while the intercept generally changes. Predictor choice should follow the scientific question and the intended interpretation.

For more than two predictors, VIFⱼ = 1 / (1 − Rⱼ²), where Rⱼ² comes from regressing Xⱼ on all the other predictors. These variance comparisons assume a correct linear mean, full rank, and constant, uncorrelated error variance.
